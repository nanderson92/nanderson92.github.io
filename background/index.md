<section class="technical-summary-panel" aria-label="Technical summary">
 <p class="system-label small">TECHNICAL SUMMARY</p>
 <h2>I am a Georgia Tech chemical engineering student focused on turning early technical signals into repeatable process decisions.</h2>
 <p>My work sits around wet processing, micromodular printed electronics, microfluidic biotech, reliability testing, image-derived metrics, and embedded process control. The common thread is not the material system; it is the operating-window habit: define what matters, measure it consistently, and decide what the next process change should be.</p>
 <p class="credential-line"><strong>Credential anchors:</strong> Georgia Tech Chemical &amp; Biomolecular Engineering · Filler Lab micromodular electronics research · Mayo Clinic microencapsulation and assay-development work · Founder and President, Georgia Tech Nanotechnology Club.</p>
</section>

 <article class="background-copy">
 <p class="system-label small">HOW I GOT HERE</p>
 <p>
 I was adopted from Incheon at seven months old and grew up in small-town Minnesota — too Korean for some rooms, not Korean enough for others. That in-between feeling made me notice systems early: who they include, who they leave out, and what technology has to become before it actually reaches people.
 </p>
 <p>
 Chemical engineering became the right language for that instinct because it treats impact as a delivery problem: transport, materials, controls, manufacturing, and scale-up are the bridge from a fragile result to something people can actually use.
 </p>
 </article>
</div>

<section class="background-depth refined-background-depth">
  <article class="featured-technical-callout">
    <p class="system-label small">AT GEORGIA TECH</p>
    <h2>I study a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication.</h2>
    <p>In Filler Lab micromodular electronics research, that concept becomes an experimental question: what substrate boundary conditions, deposition metrics, and reliability screens make printable electronics more controllable?</p>
    <p>Working knowledge of MBE deposition and SEM/XRD/XPS/Raman characterization from undergraduate semiconductor materials research also informs how I think about linking process conditions to material response.</p>
  </article>
</section>
