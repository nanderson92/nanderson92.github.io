 <article class="background-copy">
 <p class="system-label small">INCHEON → MINNESOTA → ATLANTA</p>
 <p>I was adopted from Incheon at seven months old and grew up in small-town Minnesota — too Korean for some rooms, not Korean enough for others. That in-between feeling made me notice systems early: who they include, who they leave out, and what technology has to become before it actually reaches people.</p>
 <p>The future often appears first as a fragile lab result. The harder question is what it takes to make that result reliable enough to leave the lab. That is why chemical engineering became the right language for me: transport, materials, controls, manufacturing, and scale-up as the bridge from discovery to infrastructure.</p>
 </article>

<section class="currently-thinking" aria-label="Currently thinking about">
 <p class="system-label small">CURRENTLY THINKING ABOUT</p>
 <h2>How the next wave of hardware leaves the lab.</h2>
 <p>I keep coming back to the same manufacturing question: what boundary condition, control loop, or quality check turns a fragile lab behavior into something a small team can repeat? Right now I am especially interested in printed-electronics assembly, wet-processing lines that do not require huge facilities, and biotech tools where microfluidics has to become more robust than a one-off demo.</p>
</section>

<section class="background-depth refined-background-depth">
  <article>
    <h2>At Georgia Tech</h2>
    <p>Georgia Tech gave that motivation a process-engineering direction. In my micromodular electronics work, I study a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication. The research forced me to define boundary conditions, measurable outputs, and decision rules rather than only describe what I observed.</p>
  </article>

  <article>
    <h2>Bridge to technical work</h2>
    <p>The projects on this site show that bridge in practice: extracting normalized radius traces from droplet videos, comparing substrate/backing conditions, tracking resistance drift under stress, tuning flow ratio and crosslink dwell time, and measuring ramp rate, overshoot, and hold stability.</p>
