<p class="technical-summary">I study process development for advanced manufacturing. At Georgia Tech's Filler Lab, I build measurement and decision workflows for micromodular printed electronics — a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication. Earlier work at Mayo Clinic focused on microfluidic encapsulation throughput and thermocycler automation for biological protocols.</p>

<p class="background-lede">Discovery shows what is possible. Delivery proves what can be repeated.</p>

 <h2>Why access matters</h2>
 <p>I was adopted from Incheon at seven months old and grew up in small-town Minnesota — too Korean for some rooms, not Korean enough for others. That in-between feeling made me notice systems early: who they include, who they leave out, and what technology has to become before it actually reaches people.</p>
 <p>That is why I care less about futuristic aesthetics by themselves and more about the physical systems that make advanced technology usable outside privileged rooms.</p>
 </article>
</div>

<section class="background-depth refined-background-depth three-part-background">
  <article>
    <h2>Why process engineering</h2>
    <blockquote class="technical-callout">I study a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication.</blockquote>
    <p>Georgia Tech gave that motivation a process-engineering direction. In my micromodular electronics work, the research forced me to define inputs, measurable outputs, screening rules, and failure modes instead of only describing what I observed.</p>
    <p>Working knowledge of MBE deposition and SEM/XRD/XPS/Raman characterization from undergraduate semiconductor materials research also informs how I think about linking process conditions to material response.</p>
  </article>

  <article>
    <h2>Why future infrastructure</h2>
    <p>To me, the future built environment means clean technology made physical. I care about the systems that make advanced manufacturing and low-carbon infrastructure visible in everyday life: factories, devices, energy systems, and biotech tools that people can actually use.</p>
    <p>The stories that shaped me were less about gadgets than infrastructure: interfaces, control layers, energy systems, and the physical environments people eventually live inside. Ghost in the Shell, The Matrix, and Final Fantasy VII were early references, but the lasting point was infrastructure.</p>
  </article>
</section>
