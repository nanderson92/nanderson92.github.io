<p class="background-lede">I study chemical engineering because the future only matters when it can be built, repeated, and used by real people.</p>

<section class="background-technical-summary callout" aria-label="Technical summary">
 <p class="system-label small">TECHNICAL THREAD</p>
 <h2>My work sits between fragile lab results and repeatable physical systems.</h2>
 <p>At Georgia Tech, that means process development, wet processing, micromodular printed electronics, microfluidic biotech, embedded controls, and reliability logic. The common pattern is defining boundary conditions, measurable outputs, and decision rules instead of only describing what happened.</p>
</section>

 <article class="background-copy">
 <p class="system-label small">INCHEON → MINNESOTA → ATLANTA</p>
 <p>I was adopted from Incheon at seven months old and grew up in small-town Minnesota. That in-between feeling made me notice systems early: who they include, who they leave out, and what technology has to become before it actually reaches people.</p>
 <p>The future often appears first as a fragile lab result. The harder question is what it takes to make that result reliable enough to leave the lab. That is why chemical engineering became the right language for me: transport, materials, controls, manufacturing, and scale-up as the bridge from discovery to infrastructure.</p>
 </article>
</div>

<section class="background-depth refined-background-depth">
  <article class="gt-callout-card">
    <h2>At Georgia Tech</h2>
    <p class="promoted-line">In Filler Lab, I study a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication.</p>
    <p>That work forced me to translate droplet behavior, substrate boundary conditions, deposition metrics, and reliability tests into process decisions. Working knowledge of MBE deposition and SEM/XRD/XPS/Raman characterization from undergraduate semiconductor materials research also informs how I think about linking process conditions to material response.</p>
  </article>

  <article>
    <h2>What “future built environment” means to me</h2>
    <p>To me, the future built environment means clean technology made physical. I care about the systems that make advanced manufacturing and low-carbon infrastructure visible in everyday life: factories, devices, energy systems, and biotech tools that people can actually use.</p>
  </article>
</section>

<section class="taste-panel compact-taste-panel" aria-label="Technical taste influences">
 <p class="system-label small">TECHNICAL TASTE</p>
 <h2>What shaped my taste</h2>
 <p><strong>Ghost in the Shell, The Matrix, and Final Fantasy VII</strong> made technology feel less like gadgets and more like infrastructure: interfaces, control layers, energy systems, and the physical worlds people eventually live inside. I keep that aesthetic interest below the engineering work, but it is part of why I care about making advanced technology tangible and accessible.</p>
</section>
