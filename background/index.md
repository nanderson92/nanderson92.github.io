<section class="technical-summary-band" aria-label="Technical summary">
 <p class="system-label small">TECHNICAL SUMMARY</p>
 <h2>I’m building process-development depth for physical technologies that need to leave the lab.</h2>
 <p>At Georgia Tech, my work centers on micromodular printed electronics in the Filler Lab: a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication. That work has pushed me to translate observations into boundary conditions, measurable outputs, and decision rules.</p>
</section>

 <article class="background-copy">
 <p class="system-label small">HOW I GOT HERE</p>
 <p>I was adopted from Incheon at seven months old and grew up in small-town Minnesota — too Korean for some rooms, not Korean enough for others. That in-between feeling made me notice systems early: who they include, who they leave out, and what technology has to become before it actually reaches people.</p>
 <p>The future often appears first as a fragile lab result. Chemical engineering gave me the language for the harder part: transport, materials, controls, manufacturing, and scale-up as the bridge from discovery to infrastructure.</p>
 </article>
</div>

<section class="taste-panel compact-taste-panel" aria-label="Technical taste influences">
 <p class="system-label small">TASTE / INFLUENCES</p>
 <h2>Systems, infrastructure, and technology people can actually live inside.</h2>
 <p><strong>Ghost in the Shell · The Matrix · Final Fantasy VII</strong> shaped my taste before I had engineering vocabulary for it. I started caring less about gadgets and more about the interfaces, energy systems, and physical infrastructure that decide whether technology reaches people.</p>
</section>

<section class="background-depth refined-background-depth">
  <article class="manufacturing-callout-card">
    <h2>At Georgia Tech</h2>
    <p class="large-callout">I study a manufacturing concept where semiconductor devices could be produced and assembled in a smaller, faster, less facility-intensive way than conventional fabrication.</p>
    <p>That research forced me to define boundary conditions, measurable outputs, and decision rules rather than only describe what I observed. Working knowledge of MBE deposition and SEM/XRD/XPS/Raman characterization from undergraduate semiconductor materials research also informs how I think about linking process conditions to material response.</p>
  </article>

  <article>
    <h2>What “future built environment” means to me</h2>
    <p>To me, the future built environment means clean technology made physical. I care about the systems that make advanced manufacturing and low-carbon infrastructure visible in everyday life: factories, devices, energy systems, and biotech tools that people can actually use.</p>
  </article>
