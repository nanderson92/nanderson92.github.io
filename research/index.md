## Research direction

My research interests sit at the intersection of chemical engineering, advanced manufacturing, wet processing, and scalable electronics fabrication. I am especially interested in how research-stage materials behavior can be translated into reproducible process metrics and manufacturing-relevant decision rules.

<div class="translation-strip">
  <div>
    <h3>Research Signal</h3>
    <p>Wetting, evaporation, imbibition, pinning, transport, device motion, resistance drift, and optical observations.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Engineering Metric</h3>
    <p>Radius, contact angle, volume, deposition uniformity, edge bias, reliability curves, and process-window descriptors.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h3>Manufacturing Rule</h3>
    <p>Repeatable deposition logic, routing compatibility, substrate selection, process stability, and manufacturability checks.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Why it matters

For advanced manufacturing, research behavior becomes useful when it can be measured, repeated, controlled, and scaled. My current research direction focuses on building that translation layer: from lab-scale droplet phenomena and device behavior to process metrics that help guide printed electronics assembly.
